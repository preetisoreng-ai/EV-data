Selected question: **Where is the global EV transition already large enough to affect energy demand, and how concentrated is that impact?** It uses both datasets without double-counting regions and keeps the distinction between current fleet scale and recent sales momentum.

## Main observations

- The supplied World row reports 90,579,665 EVs, 235,608.4 GWh/year of electricity demand, and 525,132.7 thousand barrels of oil equivalent displaced per year.
- China reports 51,783,437 EVs: 57.2% of the World fleet row. Its 304,870.5 thousand boe/year of reported oil displacement is 58.1% of the World row.
- The five largest country fleets—China, United States, Germany, United Kingdom, and France—sum to 77.7% of the World fleet row and 79.1% of reported World oil displacement.
- Current scale and current sales momentum are different. China pairs the largest fleet with a 67.0% monthly EV registration share in 2026-09. The United States has the second-largest fleet but an 8.21% share in the same month. Norway reports a 99.01% share in 2026-10 but a much smaller fleet of 1.26 million.

## Interpretive boundary

The concentration finding is a descriptive comparison of supplied values. The files do not document the impact model, source publisher, policy context, vehicle use, grid mix, or counterfactual oil consumption. The Original page therefore says the dataset “reports” or “associates” impact rather than claiming observed causal displacement.
